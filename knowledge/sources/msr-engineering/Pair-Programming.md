Pair programming is a great tool you can leverage to build a better solution and team. This page is to outline an approach that you could take to understand if the tool of paired programming could be useful to your execution. These are guidelines only and you should feel free to experiment with what works for the project/partners. This is a working document and can be developed as we learn what works for our team.  

##Benefits to Pair/Mob programming:
- Better understanding of application/services
- More perspectives to build from
- Team building through shared learning
- Catches more bugs before check in
- Catches design flaws before heavy investment

##Misconceptions:
Reference: [The Costs and Benefits of Pair Programming](https://collaboration.csc.ncsu.edu/laurie/Papers/XPSardinia.PDF)
- Too expensive. Two people, twice the time.
  - There is a 15% cost to paired programming. This is recovered by reduced number of bugs and passing test cases.
- Learning to program is a independent task
  - The above shows that learning in pairs is actually preferred by most 
- Code is personal and it could trigger defensive behavior
  - This could be the case but is a personal development opportunity

##Pair programming requirements
- A well defined problem
- Two or more people interested in contributing
- Neutral meeting space
- Neutral laptop for development. This is located in Trevor's office to the right of his guest chair.  Check it out on his board.
- Timer and defined rotation time. Timer is in the laptop bag.
- Enough time scheduled to complete task

## Habits to avoid
- Not having fun. If it isn't fun maybe it isn't the right solution.
- Somebody trying to take over for driver or navigator ('Let me show you')
- Not the right task
- Not switching roles
- lack of individual investment. If it isn't valuable you can leave at any time.

##Roles
<br>

**Navigator** - Focuses on the plan of action and guides the Driver's input. The navigator is also responsible for assessing the audience's guidance and translating them into the driver's actions.

**Driver** - Follows the navigator's instructions.

**Audience** - Follows the execution of the driver in preparation of assuming the role. They also communicate with the navigator to help them understand the path forward.

##Bootstrapping

To on board paired programming as a team it is recommended that you approach it as a 'mob' rather than 'pair'.  It is also recommended that you choose a problem that you already understand the solution so you're focused on learning the process of paired programming not solving the technical solution. You should agree on how frequently you rotate and should target a time that allows progress and all interested parties to contribute. This could be difficult with a large mob. If this is the case you may want to have two sessions or set the expectation that many will only play the audience role.
